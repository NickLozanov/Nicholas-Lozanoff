# Perfect Competition: Decision Memo

Supporting evidence: [Stage 3 analysis](../../analysis/2026-10-28-perfect-competition-analysis.md).

## The plan

Recommended beds per crop: **10 tomato, 20 carrot, 30 mesclun (60 of 64 beds; 4 temporary workers). Seasonal profit $42,775.16 (Summary sheet).**

## Rational and reasoning

- Tomatoes stop on marginal cost: bed 11 loses $590.17. See [Figure 1](../../analysis/figures/figure-1-tomato-mc.png) in the analysis figures.
- Carrots and mesclun stop at their caps with MC still under price: one more bed would add $353.10 and $246.57. See [Figure 2](../../analysis/figures/figure-2-mesclun-mc.png).
- The 4 idle beds are not worth planting under current caps: the only crop with room is tomato, and its next bed loses money.
- Labor has 1,203.2 spare hours; <ins>labor is not what limits the plan.</ins>

## The judgment call

- Carrot cap is worth more per bed to relax: +$353.10 vs +$246.57 for mesclun, declining with each bed.
- Caps can be lifted to carrot 24 / mesclun 34 before the 64-bed farm binds. With both lifted, the best mix is 10/23/31 (+$1,140.82); only then is extra ground worth buying, at about $212.55 for a 65th bed.
- A 5th temporary worker is worth $0 (1,203 hours slack). The 4th worker is worth keeping: losing it costs $551.89 (re-solved with 3 workers).

## What would change the answer

**Sensitivity evidence (re-solved with one price changed, others at base):**

- Tomato price: the mix holds at 10 tomato beds only between about $8,248 and $9,390 per bed. A fall of 6.3% drops it to 9 beds. A 20% fall ($7,040) gives 8/20/30 and profit $26,586.64 (negative $16,188.51).
- Mesclun price: 30 beds hold down to about $2,420, a 10.4% fall. A 20% fall gives 10/20/21, profit $27,780.19.
- Carrot price: 20 beds hold down to about $1,688, a 19.4% fall. A 20% fall gives 10/19/30, profit $34,412.32.
- Tomato price is the variable the plan is most sensitive to: smallest change to move the mix and largest profit swing.
